# Painting a place the player can walk around

In the PAL N64 Kakariko shooting gallery, the roof uses a 16 × 32 tile, the roof edge a 32 × 32 tile, the wall a 32 × 64 tile and the shadow around the door a 16 × 32 tile. The sign has its own 32 × 32 picture. A photograph of that building looks detailed because each picture fits a particular part of the building. The same approach works on a Bellwake cottage when the player can stand close enough to look under its eaves. [Shooting-gallery asset declarations](https://github.com/zeldaret/oot/blob/14759abf9abb4295de142cdc1452998630ea70fb/assets/xml/objects/object_spot01_matoya.xml)

## Houses, shops and furniture

**Start with the opening.** Build a wall, recessed door, jambs, lintel and step at player scale. Leave a little of the dark return visible when the door is viewed at an angle. Paint the narrow front of a lintel lengthwise. Give a roof's exposed edge its own horizontal strip with terracotta ends, a narrow shadow and pegged wood below. Keep the stone on the top and face of a threshold continuous through its bevel. The 04:52:01 view in the recording is a useful framing test. The doorway stands out in a small stone room even though the floor and wall have similar brightness. Bellmarket's cottage fascia, doorframes and steps each have fitted artwork. [Bellwake cottage paintings and Blender fitting](../../Tools/bellwake/cottage_edges.py)

**Shop signs.** In the inspected Kakariko bazaar sign, two triangles use one complete 64 × 64 CI4 picture. Two triangles on the potion-shop sign use a 32 × 16 RGBA16 picture. Aspect ratio does much of the work. Build a projecting bracket and a thin sign with thickness, paint the emblem onto each broad face and give the narrow edge a wood finish. On the Bellwake provisioner's stall, the hem, bracket, suspension cords and enamel sign all have depth. Close one eye and lean sideways to check the cords clear the board. **A window can show a business.** Original Kakariko selects a day or night window image with the save clock. Both images are 32 × 64. The potion and mask shops have a different construction, with 256 × 256 indexed background panels and palettes. At 01:03:03 the shelf goods frame a merchant's face. At 05:57:03 the curtains and the masks belong to the same shop composition. In VR, put a small original interior painting behind a real sill, crossbars and shutters. In Bellwake, a lamp brightens the colours already in the picture. Dark vessels and shelves keep their silhouettes at night. **Books and drawers.** A little painted rectangle means more when it has a physical ledge above and below it. Keep the spines as separate solids, tint the bindings by shelf, map the decorated leather onto the narrow outward face and leave the page edges pale. Reuse that sheet for a recessed drawer panel or an index cabinet with a different piece of artwork. Turn your head across a whole shelf. The ledges should move against the bindings, while the gilding remains attached to the leather. Flood Ledger, Vellum Walk, Linen Court and the parish office use this layout. ## What happens to the paintings at a doorway

The N64 room manager calculates the largest single room and the largest pair sharing a transition. It reserves enough space for whichever is larger. A new request saves the previous room, reads the next one asynchronously, then runs its scene commands. Finishing the change clears the previous room reference, updates actors and applies the new room’s reverb. The artwork at a doorway matters because the two neighbouring rooms can share that buffer. ## Sources and scope
